# Gameplay

**Status:** Canonical loop
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core loop

1. **Form an intent:** Decide what experience, customer, commission, or house need the next cigar should serve.
2. **Inspect the pipeline:** Review crops, leaf lots, active processes, aging inventory, current releases, and upcoming commitments.
3. **Grow or source:** Cultivate estate tobacco and build relationships to acquire complementary leaf from named growers.
4. **Develop the leaf:** Cure, sort, ferment, rest, and age lots while preserving provenance and responding to their changing condition.
5. **Design:** Choose blend components, construction, vitola, and intended production method.
6. **Prototype:** Prepare and roll a small number of test cigars.
7. **Evaluate:** Observe construction and sensory results, compare them with the intent, and diagnose causes.
8. **Revise:** Change leaf, ratios, processing, construction, or expectations and test again.
9. **Commit:** Approve a specification, allocate finite leaf, and choose atelier or house production.
10. **Produce and mature:** Make the batch, inspect it, age it, package it, and decide when it is ready.
11. **Release:** Select buyers, channels, quantities, presentation, and promises.
12. **Learn:** Interpret reactions, financial results, process history, and relationships, then carry that knowledge into the next decision.

This is not a linear conveyor belt. Several crops, lots, prototypes, production batches, and releases overlap across the calendar.

## Three connected scales

### The hands

The player directly inspects materials, develops blends, constructs prototypes, diagnoses flaws, and teaches difficult work.

### The house

The player establishes standards, allocates leaf, trains named artisans, improves facilities, schedules work, and protects consistency.

### The name

The player maintains relationships, fulfills commitments, releases cigars, and develops a recognizable reputation among different audiences.

Major features should connect at least two of these scales.

## Farming loop

Farming supports the cigar-making loop:

1. Understand the tendencies and constraints of each plot.
2. Choose seed, cultivation method, and intended use.
3. Prepare, plant, observe, and respond to the crop.
4. Decide harvest timing by plant position and condition.
5. Cure, sort, and grade the resulting leaf.
6. Retain, sell, trade, contract, or process each lot further.
7. Learn how the season, land, and decisions affected the result.

The goal is expressive estate tobacco, not maximum field count. Routine care becomes delegable, while diagnosis, experiments, exceptional plots, and harvest decisions remain meaningful.

## Production paths

- **Atelier production:** prototypes, rare lots, numbered batches, bespoke work, and cigars personally made or closely led by the player.
- **House production:** established cigars produced by trained artisans according to recorded specifications and tolerances.

The player discovers excellence; the house learns to reproduce it. See [`docs/design/CRAFT_AND_GROWTH.md`](docs/design/CRAFT_AND_GROWTH.md).

## Daily rhythm

The preferred day is deliberate rather than frantic:

1. Review conditions, commitments, and upcoming checkpoints.
2. Select the work that deserves personal attention.
3. Delegate routine or standardized tasks.
4. Perform hands-on work, training, sourcing, planning, or relationship activities.
5. Record observations and make allocation decisions.
6. End or deliberately advance time with visibility into what comes next.

Movement itself should not create constant time pressure. Problems develop through readable warning states and offer opportunities for diagnosis and recovery.

## Goal

Build a cigar house worth remembering. Success may mean a revered atelier, a reliable premium portfolio, exceptional estate tobacco, experimental mastery, respected artisans, or one defining cigar. Production volume alone is not victory.

## First meaningful playable

The first meaningful playable must demonstrate the game's actual identity, even if it uses authored test lots and accelerated processes:

1. Inspect two or more provenance-rich leaf lots.
2. Define an intended cigar.
3. Build and roll a prototype.
4. Receive understandable construction and sensory feedback.
5. Revise at least one meaningful choice.
6. Commit and produce a tiny batch.
7. Release it to a buyer or evaluator.
8. Advance time and receive a reaction that affects knowledge, money, access, or reputation.

A farm-only grow-and-harvest loop is a useful subsystem test, but it is not the vertical slice for Leaf & Ember.
