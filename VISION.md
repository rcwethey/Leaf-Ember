# Leaf & Ember vision

**Status:** Canonical
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## North star

> Leaf & Ember is an intimate craftsmanship and management RPG about building a legendary cigar house through patient agriculture, thoughtful leaf selection, precise construction, distinctive blends, trusted relationships, and an earned reputation.

The player begins with their own hands on every important step. Growth gives them the people, facilities, and knowledge to delegate established work without making personal craftsmanship obsolete.

## Player fantasy

The player acquires a neglected small finca and cigar workshop in a fictional highland region inspired primarily by northern Nicaragua. The place has a past, but the player inherits no reputation, famous recipe, or predetermined identity. They must create a house worth remembering.

The player is simultaneously:

- A grower learning what their land can express
- A leaf buyer building relationships with other growers
- A blender pursuing particular experiences
- A craftsperson learning cigar construction
- A mentor establishing standards and training named artisans
- A proprietor deciding what the house will value

## Emotional promise

Leaf & Ember should make small achievements feel significant: the first sound leaf from a difficult harvest, a corrected prototype, twenty cigars accepted by a buyer, an apprentice mastering a technique, or a mature release finally expressing its intended character.

The tone is grounded, warm, mature, and atmospheric. The game should value observation, patience, judgment, human skill, and pride in work over frantic optimization.

## Design pillars

### Craftsmanship over scale

Success comes from understanding leaf, process, blend, construction, and people. More acreage and higher output are tools, not the objective.

### Growth without displacement

Expansion delegates repetition. The player's hands remain relevant for experimentation, diagnosis, teaching, rare materials, bespoke work, and masterwork releases throughout the game.

### The finca is a living pipeline

Fields, curing barns, fermentation, conditioning, rolling, aging, packaging, and administration are physically represented on the estate. Tobacco moves through these spaces and carries its history with it.

### The farm gives the player a voice, not a complete vocabulary

The finca's terroir creates characteristic tendencies, not one fixed flavor. Player choices can develop that range, while relationships with other growers provide the complementary leaves required for broader blending.

### Patience without empty waiting

Processes unfold across days, seasons, and years, but the player maintains overlapping crops, leaf lots, prototypes, production batches, aging stock, relationships, and releases. Time creates decisions instead of dead space.

### Reputation is earned and specific

There is no universal prestige meter that makes everyone agree. Growers, artisans, buyers, critics, collectors, and the local community value different behavior. The house becomes known for what it repeatedly makes and how it conducts itself.

### People are not production multipliers

Workers are named artisans with specialties, habits, ambitions, relationships, and developing mastery. A great house is also a lineage of people capable of carrying its standards forward.

## Three scales of play

- **The hands:** Inspect, sort, blend, roll, taste, diagnose, and teach.
- **The house:** Establish specifications, train artisans, manage facilities, allocate leaf, and preserve consistency.
- **The name:** Build relationships, release cigars, serve markets, and develop a recognizable identity.

Every major system should strengthen at least one scale and preferably connect two or more.

## Valid long-term identities

The game must support multiple respected outcomes:

- A tiny, revered atelier producing numbered batches
- A respected regional house with a consistent portfolio
- A larger premium producer that preserves demanding standards
- An experimental blender known for unusual expressions
- An estate recognized for exceptional tobacco
- A mentor whose trained artisans become part of the house's legacy
- The creator of one defining cigar remembered for years

These are identities, not mutually exclusive victory screens.

## Explicit non-goals

Leaf & Ember is not intended to become:

- An acreage-maximizing farming game
- A generic factory-automation game
- A business tycoon where money is the only meaningful score
- A crafting system where ingredients collapse into one quality number
- A progression ladder where later ingredients are strictly superior
- A collection of encyclopedic facts without atmosphere or human context
- A frantic daily checklist built around punishment for missing arbitrary timers

## Decision test

Before adding a major feature, ask:

1. Does it deepen judgment, expression, relationships, or stewardship?
2. Does it connect the player's hands, house, or name?
3. Does it preserve meaningful choice after delegation becomes available?
4. Does it respect the finca's intimate scale?
5. Does it create understandable consequences rather than opaque punishment?

If a feature fails these tests, it needs revision or does not belong in the game.
