# Blending and recipes

**Status:** Canonical direction; balance formulas remain open
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Governing model

```text
leaf potential
* process condition
* blend interactions
* vitola and construction
= cigar expression over time
```

A blend is a physical design made from finite, particular tobacco. It is not a list of flavor points or a recipe that ignores format and execution.

## Development workflow

1. Define the intended experience, audience, occasion, and constraints.
2. Choose a vitola and intended atelier or house production path.
3. Select candidate wrapper, binder, and filler lots.
4. Decide proportions and placement within the bunch.
5. Review predicted ranges, resource cost, and uncertainty.
6. Prepare and construct several prototypes.
7. Rest them to a useful testing state.
8. Taste, compare, diagnose, and revise.
9. Approve a version and convert it into a production specification.

## Information at the blending table

For each known lot, the player can review:

- Provenance and harvest
- Available and committed quantity
- Current physical condition
- Strength, body, and aroma-family observations
- Combustion behavior
- Elasticity and likely role suitability
- Processing and aging history
- Knowledge confidence
- Cost and opportunity value

Reported claims and predictions remain visibly distinct from the player's direct observations.

## Component roles

- **Filler:** Establishes much of the cigar's internal volume, combustion behavior, strength, body, and central character.
- **Binder:** Holds the bunch, supports shape and combustion, and can contribute meaningful sensory character.
- **Wrapper:** Provides the outer structure and finish and can contribute differently depending on the leaf, blend, and wrapper-to-filler proportion.

The game must not enforce one universal percentage for how much flavor each role contributes. Material, format, and construction determine the result.

## Interaction types

Components can interact through:

- **Reinforcement:** Similar characteristics become more prominent.
- **Layering:** Related characteristics create depth.
- **Contrast:** Opposing qualities make one another more noticeable.
- **Bridging:** One component connects otherwise separated characteristics.
- **Masking:** An intense component obscures something subtle.
- **Conflict:** Several dominant characteristics compete and become muddy.
- **Structural support:** A leaf improves combustion or body without becoming the dominant flavor.

Interactions must arise from understandable component traits and thresholds. Do not create arbitrary secret combinations that grant special bonuses.

## Hybrid player control

During prototype development, the player handles whole or partial leaves and makes physical choices about selection, amount, orientation, and placement. The notebook converts those actions into clear proportions and a reproducible specification.

This preserves tactile craft without requiring the player to perform manual arithmetic or track leaf fractions outside the game.

## Prediction

Before construction, the system presents a range rather than a result:

> Likely medium-to-full strength with pronounced earth and roast. Moderate possibility of dark sweetness. Final behavior uncertain because this filler combination has not been tested in this format.

Prediction confidence depends on experience with the lots, related harvests, the vitola, and comparable recipes. Better knowledge narrows uncertainty but never replaces prototype testing.

## Recipe notebook

Every version records:

- Name and version identifier
- Design intent
- Exact source lots and proportions
- Vitola and dimensions
- Construction method and target compression
- Tobacco condition targets
- Required prototype rest
- Tasting records
- Diagnosis and revision rationale
- Material cost and available production quantity

The notebook shows meaningful differences between versions and preserves failed experiments as knowledge.

## Production specification

An approved prototype becomes a specification containing:

- Mandatory lots and acceptable substitutions
- Blend proportions and placement
- Vitola, method, and target dimensions
- Weight, draw, moisture, and construction tolerances
- Resting and aging requirements
- Inspection frequency and rejection rules
- Maximum batch size
- Required artisan qualifications

The specification describes intent and tolerances. Every actual batch retains its own material and maker history.

## Scale and substitution

A remarkable cigar may resist scaling because its leaf is scarce, the construction is difficult, or its tolerances are demanding. The player may:

- Keep it as an atelier release
- Reduce batch size
- Reserve future crops
- Train specialists
- Redesign for more attainable material
- Approve a carefully tested substitution

Substitution is a new design decision, not an invisible inventory convenience. A house line earns trust by remaining recognizably itself even when agriculture makes perfect repetition impossible.

## Design constraints

- Do not average component flavor vectors and call the result complete.
- Do not reveal an exact final profile before testing.
- Do not let recipes consume unavailable or multiply committed lots.
- Do not make the most expensive component automatically best.
- Preserve recipe version history and source-lot provenance.
- Let format and construction change how the same composition expresses itself.
