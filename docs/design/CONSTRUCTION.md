# Cigar construction

**Status:** Canonical direction; exact gestures and method roster remain open
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rule

Construction is deliberate, tactile craft rather than a rhythm game. The player makes material and physical decisions, and the simulation produces a cigar with measurable structure rather than an arbitrary rolling score.

## Full personal-craft sequence

### 1. Condition the leaves

Bring each component to workable moisture and elasticity. Material condition affects handling, compression, structural integrity, and later combustion.

### 2. Prepare the components

Inspect, orient, trim, and remove the appropriate portion of central veins while preserving useful leaf area.

### 3. Arrange the filler

Choose component placement and fold or arrange filler to preserve continuous airflow. The player must account for strength, burn behavior, dimensions, and the intended progression.

### 4. Form the bunch

Control material distribution and compression along the cigar's length. The goal is not maximum tightness but an even structure appropriate to the specification.

### 5. Apply the binder

Establish the bunch's structure, shape, and target diameter without twisting or obstructing its internal pathways.

### 6. Mold and press

Choose mold, pressure, duration, and any rotation between pressing stages. This sets shape without correcting a fundamentally bad bunch.

### 7. Prepare the wrapper

Select orientation and cut around veins, defects, and visual variation while using the leaf efficiently.

### 8. Apply the wrapper

Maintain appropriate and consistent tension without tearing, overstretching, or leaving loose sections.

### 9. Create the cap and trim

Secure the head, complete the cap, and cut the cigar to its intended length.

### 10. Inspect and rest

Record dimensions, weight, firmness, visible defects, maker, recipe version, and required recovery time.

## Simulation result

The construction system records:

- Density distribution
- Airflow continuity
- Compression consistency
- Component placement
- Wrapper tension
- Seam and cap integrity
- Shape and dimensions
- Moisture distribution
- Material damage

These properties contribute to draw, burn, smoke delivery, appearance, handling, and batch consistency.

## Diagnosable faults

Possible symptoms include:

- Tight or obstructed draw
- Loose draw and overly rapid combustion
- Uneven burn
- Soft or excessively dense sections
- Unstable or excessive smoke temperature
- Wrapper splitting or unraveling
- Poor cap or visible finishing defects
- Significant variation among cigars from one batch

Some faults are visible or measurable immediately. Others emerge only after resting and smoking. The player must connect symptoms to possible material, conditioning, bunching, pressing, or wrapping causes.

## Interaction design

The interface uses deliberate positioning, shaping, pressure, and cutting gestures with clear visual, tactile, and audio feedback. It must not depend on fast reaction prompts or pixel-perfect tracing.

Mouse, controller, and keyboard-assisted inputs must express equivalent decisions. Assistance can stabilize gestures without choosing the blend, material condition, construction method, or diagnosis for the player.

## Skill and mastery

Construction mastery improves:

- **Control:** The ability to achieve narrower tolerances
- **Perception:** Earlier recognition of uneven density, moisture, tension, or alignment
- **Understanding:** Better explanations of likely causes and corrective actions
- **Range:** Access to more demanding formats and methods
- **Teaching:** The ability to communicate a standard to another artisan

Skill does not apply a universal quality bonus. A master can still make a poor cigar from unsuitable material, a flawed specification, or a careless decision.

## Levels of involvement

- **Full craft:** The player performs every construction stage for prototypes, instruction, and masterwork cigars.
- **Assisted craft:** Routine preparation is handled while the player controls critical stages.
- **Supervised craft:** A named artisan works while the player observes, inspects, and intervenes.
- **House production:** Trained staff follow the approved specification and quality tolerances.

The game must never require the player to roll a commercial batch one cigar at a time. Repetition belongs to the house once the player has established a teachable standard.

## Artisan simulation

Named artisans use the same construction model. Their specialties, experience, familiarity with a recipe, current material condition, fatigue, workshop environment, and instruction affect their ability to meet tolerances.

Training should target observed causes rather than filling a generic experience bar. An artisan may be excellent with traditional straight-sided formats but inexperienced with a difficult shape, wrapper, or bunching method.

## Quality control

Immediate inspection may include:

- Dimensions and weight
- Firmness and soft spots
- Visible wrapper and cap condition
- Draw testing where available
- Sampled internal or destructive inspection
- Batch variance

Smoking tests remain necessary because a cigar can satisfy visible measurements and still fail its sensory intent.

## Production specification

Construction requirements are versioned with the blend and include method, material condition, placement, dimensions, target weight, draw range, tolerances, pressing, rest, inspection rate, and required artisan qualification.

## Design constraints

- Never use a single rolling score as the source of quality.
- Do not hide faults until after a whole batch is produced when earlier evidence should exist.
- Preserve meaningful differences between player execution, artisan execution, recipe design, and material condition.
- Support optional input assistance without reducing the craft to automatic success.
- Keep the full interaction compact enough for repeated prototyping.
