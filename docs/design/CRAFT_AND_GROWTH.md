# Craft and growth

**Status:** Canonical direction
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rule

> Growth expands the player's choices; it does not remove the player from the craft.

At the beginning, the player personally handles nearly every important task because the house has no established standards or trained team. Later, named artisans reproduce proven work while the player chooses where their own attention matters most.

## Two production paths

| Atelier production | House production |
| --- | --- |
| Personally crafted or closely led by the player | Produced by trained staff to an approved specification |
| Prototypes, experiments, rare lots, and bespoke work | Established portfolio cigars and recurring orders |
| Tiny, often numbered batches | Reliable commercial quantities |
| Maximum creative control and diagnostic detail | Greater consistency, reach, and resilience |
| Builds the maker's personal prestige | Builds confidence in the cigar house |
| Accepts unusual methods and demanding construction | Requires teachable and repeatable standards |

Neither path is inherently superior. A mature house may depend on both.

## Progression of responsibility

### Founder

The player inspects, sorts, blends, rolls, ages, packages, and sells nearly everything. Progress comes from learning what good work requires.

### Working master

The player brings in a small number of helpers or apprentices, demonstrates techniques, and begins recording recipes and tolerances. Delegation is limited by the player's ability to explain and teach.

### House builder

Named artisans take responsibility for established lines. The player manages leaf allocation, facilities, training, quality control, and relationships while reserving personal time for development and exceptional work.

### Master of the house

The player chooses freely between leadership and the workbench. They can direct a respected portfolio, mentor specialists, or devote substantial time to small releases without making either approach a failure state.

These stages describe changing responsibilities, not mandatory business size.

## Why the player returns to the workbench

Late-game personal craft remains valuable for:

- Developing and testing new blends
- Working with unusually rare or expressive leaf
- Producing commemorative or numbered releases
- Fulfilling a bespoke commission
- Diagnosing construction or consistency failures
- Teaching an artisan a difficult technique
- Rescuing a promising but flawed batch
- Creating a personal master's selection

The game must not reduce this to a permanent percentage bonus applied whenever the player repeats a minigame. Personal involvement provides information, expression, teaching, and access to demanding work.

## Teaching standards

The player does not delegate a cigar by assigning workers to a progress bar. They define and teach a production specification that may include:

- Blend composition and acceptable substitutions
- Tobacco condition at assembly
- Vitola and target dimensions
- Leaf placement and bunching method
- Wrapper preparation and finishing expectations
- Target draw and combustion tolerances
- Resting and aging requirements
- Inspection and rejection standards
- Batch size and acceptable variance

The player discovers excellence. The house learns to reproduce it.

A cigar can be excellent but difficult to manufacture consistently. The player may simplify it, accept lower output, train specialists, improve the workshop, tighten leaf selection, or keep it exclusive to atelier production.

## Named artisans

Artisans have more than a generic skill level. Over time they can develop:

- Construction specialties
- Sensory and diagnostic ability
- Speed versus precision tendencies
- Comfort with particular vitolas or leaf conditions
- Teaching and leadership ability
- Confidence, habits, and personal ambitions
- Relationships with the player and one another

Mastery should be visible in work and behavior. A trained artisan carrying a house standard forward is part of the player's legacy.

Recruitment, employment, learning, ambition, conflict, and departure are governed by [`CHARACTERS_AND_RELATIONSHIPS.md`](CHARACTERS_AND_RELATIONSHIPS.md).

## Expansion

Meaningful expansion emphasizes capability:

- Better environmental control
- More specialized curing and fermentation spaces
- Deeper aging reserves
- Improved sorting and recordkeeping
- Additional workstations and trained artisans
- A packaging identity and reliable distribution
- Contracts and trust with excellent growers

Land expansion may occur, but the game must not imply that owning every source is the ideal endpoint. A compact estate with exceptional facilities, people, and relationships is a complete and prestigious playstyle.

## Supported identities

The progression system must allow the player to remain a tiny atelier, develop a balanced boutique house, establish a larger premium operation, specialize in experimental releases, become known for estate tobacco, or focus on mentorship.

Progress should measure expanded capability and earned trust rather than forcing every player toward the same production volume.
