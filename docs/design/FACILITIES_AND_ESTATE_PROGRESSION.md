# Facilities and estate progression

**Status:** Canonical direction; exact layout, values, and construction timing remain open
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rules

> Improving the finca expands control, knowledge, resilience, and character rather than merely making every production number larger.

> Model meaningful consequences; compress routine procedure.

The finca is the physical expression of the house the player is building. Restoration should reveal its history, make the production route legible, and create new decisions without turning Leaf & Ember into a construction, maintenance, or industrial-logistics game.

## Game-first abstraction

The complete facility vocabulary describes what the world represents. It is not a promise that every real function receives a separate building, interface, minigame, or maintenance schedule.

A function deserves a distinct player interaction only when it creates a distinct decision. Otherwise it can be:

- Represented visually inside a shared room
- Accessed through a shared workstation
- Recorded in a lot's provenance and process history
- Scheduled as a policy or work order
- Performed by a named character
- Resolved during deliberate time advancement

The player personally handles inspection, diagnosis, experiments, important interventions, and expressive work. Routine handling becomes schedulable or delegable. Passive processes advance to readable checkpoints instead of demanding constant adjustments.

The intended full game, first shipped implementation, and vertical slice may use different degrees of compression while preserving the same underlying concepts.

## Initial condition

The finca begins neglected but viable. It is neither a blank construction grid nor a total ruin. The property has a recognizable layout, useful history, several salvageable buildings, and enough recoverable capability to produce one honest cigar before major expansion.

The initial estate includes:

- A modest house and office with incomplete historical records
- Several small field plots, only one of which is immediately ready for cultivation
- A weathered but repairable curing barn
- A basic receiving and sorting area
- A small fermentation area unable to handle sensitive or numerous lots reliably
- A conditioning and preparation room requiring cleanup
- A workshop with one recoverable rolling station
- A small aging space with unstable conditions
- Packaging and storage sharing inadequate space
- Unreliable drainage, ventilation, electricity, or water in parts of the property
- A courtyard that can become the finca's natural social center

The opening provides a small amount of usable or purchased leaf. The player does not need to wait for an entire crop cycle before inspecting tobacco, prototyping, and pursuing the inaugural release.

## Restoration sequence

### Stabilize the property

Stop immediate leaks or hazards, clean critical areas, restore basic utilities, and protect existing tobacco and equipment.

### Establish a minimum viable workshop

Make leaf inspection and conditioning, one rolling bench, developmental evaluation, and short-term cigar resting dependable enough for a tiny inaugural batch.

### Reconnect the estate crop

Restore one field plot and part of the curing barn so estate tobacco can enter the long pipeline while the player continues sourcing complementary leaf.

### Gain process control

Improve conditioning, fermentation, monitoring, sorting, storage, aging, and records according to the house's needs.

### Build a coherent house

Create appropriate spaces for artisans, quality control, reserves, packaging, visitors, and administration.

### Express a specialization

Invest toward an atelier, estate-tobacco focus, experimental house, reliable boutique producer, teaching workshop, or another supported identity.

The first two stages provide a short authored opening. Afterward, restoration order branches. A player can prioritize the curing barn, fermentation, aging, workshop, packaging, or infrastructure according to present risks and long-term intent.

## Estate structure

The finca uses a semi-authored footprint.

- Existing buildings occupy intentional locations and carry visible history.
- Rooms can be restored, equipped, and repurposed within credible limits.
- Workstations and storage can be arranged inside suitable spaces.
- A limited set of wings, partitions, and outbuildings can be added at defined locations.
- Field plots remain modest and individually legible.
- The player cannot erase the estate and replace it with an enormous factory grid.

This approach preserves atmosphere, reliable navigation, authored scenes, and a readable material route while giving different houses meaningful spatial identities.

## Layout effects

Layout matters at the scale of operational judgment, not doorway optimization.

- Receiving should connect naturally to sorting and agricultural handling.
- Fermentation, conditioning, and leaf storage need sensible material movement.
- Clean rolling and packaging should be separated from dirtier agricultural work.
- Aging benefits from environmental stability and limited unnecessary traffic.
- Records should remain accessible to production decisions.
- Social spaces should exist where people would naturally gather.

Poor layout can add handling time, congestion, schedule conflicts, or modest material risk. It must not create fragile worker pathfinding puzzles or force the player to optimize every meter of travel.

## Operating model

Each active production space has five core operating qualities:

| Quality | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Condition | Structural integrity, cleanliness, and equipment health |
| Load | Current material and work relative to comfortable capacity |
| Stability | Ability to resist weather and external changes |
| Control | Ability to deliberately alter relevant conditions |
| Visibility | Accuracy and timeliness of observations and warnings |

Projects can also improve workflow, working conditions, safety, and resilience without collapsing those effects into one facility level.

### Relevant environment only

Spaces simulate only factors that matter to the work performed there:

- Temperature
- Moisture or humidity
- Airflow
- Cleanliness or contamination risk

A curing barn, fermentation area, and aging room do not need identical controls. An office does not need production-environment gameplay at all.

Weather, building condition, material load, equipment, and player policy influence the resulting conditions. The exact mathematical model remains prototype work.

### Readable states

Environmental change is communicated through trends and warnings:

```text
stable -> trending -> at risk -> damaging
```

Early observations may be qualitative or uncertain. Better instruments and more experienced people improve visibility. They reveal problems sooner or more precisely rather than granting an invisible universal bonus.

### Player actions

Meaningful facility actions include:

- Inspecting or measuring a space
- Adjusting a broad operating profile
- Opening or restricting ventilation where appropriate
- Moving, separating, or reducing material
- Assigning someone to monitor a sensitive process
- Scheduling maintenance or repair
- Intervening when a warning becomes significant

The game should not require continuous exact-slider adjustments. Stable rooms can follow recorded policies until conditions or work change.

## Capacity

Capacity is normally a soft limit.

- Below comfortable capacity, the room is easier to organize and control.
- Near capacity, it loses flexibility and may require more attention.
- Above capacity, congestion, variation, working-condition problems, and environmental risk increase.
- A hard limit is reserved for physically impossible or unsafe situations.

Visible overload creates choices. The player may accept temporary risk, move a lot, delay work, sell inventory, rent an outside capability, change the schedule, or fund an expansion.

Capacity never functions alone. Additional space without equipment, staff, utilities, or standards is not automatically useful production capacity.

## Compressed estate zones

The player-facing estate can compress the production vocabulary into six major zones.

### Fields and nursery

This zone supports seed establishment, distinct field plots, crop observation, and agricultural supplies. Routine plant care is abstracted or delegated; plot condition, experiments, and harvest decisions remain meaningful.

### Curing barn

The barn supports hanging, airflow, ventilation, inspection, and the first major transformation of estate tobacco. Additional separation allows different harvest positions, experiments, or lots with incompatible needs to be managed independently.

### Leaf house

One connected building or group of rooms can represent:

- Receiving, weighing, identification, and provenance registration
- Sorting and grading
- Fermentation and pilones
- Conditioning
- Processed-leaf storage and aging

Early functions share imperfect space and schedules. Later partitions or dedicated rooms create useful separation when the player has enough material and distinct process needs to justify it.

### Workshop

The workshop can represent:

- Leaf preparation
- The player's rolling bench
- Artisan stations
- Molds, tools, recipes, and production specifications
- Construction inspection and quality control
- Developmental tasting and diagnosis

The tasting area is a working craft space rather than a default commercial cigar lounge.

### Aging and packaging

This zone protects finished cigars through resting and longer aging, preserves reserves, and supports final inspection, bands, boxes, allocation, packaging, and shipping preparation.

Fresh production, active releases, and long-term reserves can begin in shared storage and separate only when their conflicting needs create meaningful gameplay.

### Office and courtyard

The office holds provenance, recipes, contracts, payroll, schedules, correspondence, and house history. The courtyard and shared table support meals, informal tastings, visiting growers, milestones, and character scenes.

Living quarters and practical staff spaces ground the finca but do not require a separate decorating or household-management game.

## Material route

The estate should make this journey physically understandable:

```text
field
  -> curing
  -> receiving and sorting
  -> fermentation
  -> leaf storage and aging
  -> conditioning and preparation
  -> rolling and quality control
  -> cigar resting and aging
  -> packaging and shipping
```

Every important lot retains where it went, what occurred, and who handled it. Physical movement reinforces provenance rather than functioning as a logistics puzzle.

## Specialization through separation

Early multipurpose rooms create manageable limitations: schedule conflicts, mixed storage, uncertain control, or an inability to treat two lots differently. Progress allows the player to separate a function when doing so solves a real problem.

Separation is not automatically superior. Partitioning a room can improve isolation while reducing flexible capacity. A small experimental space may offer excellent control but be unsuitable for a commercial batch. More rolling stations increase potential output only when people, storage, inspection, and demand support them.

## Construction projects

Estate changes occur through deliberate projects rather than instant building levels. A project communicates:

- Cost and payment timing
- Duration and calendar window
- Affected space and operational disruption
- Required capability or outside craftsperson
- The limitation it addresses
- Tradeoffs or new requirements
- Its physical and visual result

Project categories include:

- **Repair:** Restore damaged structure or equipment.
- **Equip:** Add racks, instruments, workstations, molds, ventilation, or storage.
- **Reconfigure:** Partition a room or change a credible use.
- **Expand:** Add limited space to an existing building.
- **Specialize:** Create an environment for a distinct process or house identity.
- **Harden:** Add drainage, backup power, storm protection, fire protection, or other resilience.

The player chooses a plan, arranges funding and labor, and operates through the disruption. Small opening repairs may be embodied interactions, but the player does not collect generic construction resources or personally manufacture every component.

Local builders, equipment suppliers, box makers, and other craftspeople can connect projects to named relationships. Friendship does not erase project cost; trust and experience can improve communication, options, planning, and recovery from problems.

## Upkeep and repair

Routine upkeep is predictable and delegable.

- The player chooses or funds an understandable maintenance standard.
- Upcoming work and costs appear in the ledger and calendar.
- Heavy use, overload, weather, and deferred work accelerate wear.
- Warnings appear before serious avoidable failures.
- Routine work can be scheduled and assigned.

Deferred maintenance first reduces stability, control, comfort, capacity, or resilience. A neglected problem can force material movement, schedule changes, or emergency spending before it causes severe loss.

The game must not surprise the player by randomly destroying irreplaceable inventory without readable cause and an opportunity to respond.

## Utilities and infrastructure

Infrastructure operates across the estate:

- Water and drainage
- Electrical service
- Ventilation support
- Communications
- Road and shipping access
- Fire and emergency protection

Projects improve these systems through understandable capabilities and resilience. Players do not manually place pipes, cables, ducts, or road segments.

A shared utility can affect several buildings, create a construction prerequisite, or produce a visible operating constraint. Redundancy protects important work rather than serving as a mandatory technology ladder.

## Staffing and attention

Every small space can initially be operated personally. Growth creates more simultaneous work than the player can monitor alone.

A functioning space requires:

- Someone capable of the work
- Enough scheduled attention
- Appropriate conditions and equipment
- Clear responsibility for inspections and interventions
- Standards or instructions when work is delegated

Named characters provide judgment, skill, and accountability rather than a generic production multiplier. Experienced people may notice a trend earlier, maintain a taught standard, handle a process independently, or explain why a room is failing.

Expanding without developing people creates unused or poorly supervised capacity. Adding people without appropriate facilities creates congestion, frustration, and inconsistency. Employment, workload, agency, and development follow [`CHARACTERS_AND_RELATIONSHIPS.md`](CHARACTERS_AND_RELATIONSHIPS.md).

## Capability paths

Estate development follows the player's intended identity rather than one universal building sequence.

| Direction | Representative investments |
| --- | --- |
| Atelier and experimentation | Flexible preparation, personal benches, micro-batch storage, developmental quality control |
| Estate tobacco | Nursery support, curing separation, grading, crop records, processed-leaf storage |
| Process mastery | Specialized fermentation, conditioning, monitoring, and long-term aging |
| House production | Artisan stations, workflow, inspection capacity, and dependable packaging |
| Teaching and legacy | Apprentice stations, demonstration space, shared standards, and supervised work |
| Market and hospitality | Packaging, shipping, private meetings, and visitor presentation without a commercial lounge requirement |

These are compatible directions, not locked classes. Money, physical space, staffing, inventory, and attention make investment order meaningful even when a long-running house eventually develops several strengths.

The city and the player's relationships can provide capabilities the finca does not own. Printing, boxes, shipping, specialized storage, equipment service, and some agricultural work can remain external. Complete self-sufficiency is not automatically ideal.

## Visible and social growth

Progress appears through use and accumulated history:

- Repaired roofs, walls, paths, drainage, lighting, and vegetation
- Increasingly organized racks, tools, labels, and records
- Tobacco visibly occupying curing, fermentation, and aging spaces
- Artisans working independently and teaching others
- Growers, craftspeople, buyers, and deliveries arriving for recognizable reasons
- Releases, boxes, photographs, awards, experiments, and mementos accumulating
- The courtyard becoming active during meals and milestones
- Former apprentices returning as peers or collaborators
- Old and new construction coexisting instead of becoming generically pristine

A renowned house can remain physically small. Deep reserves, excellent working conditions, distinctive methods, trusted people, and a documented history communicate prestige more effectively than raw building count.

## Estate progression states

These are descriptive conditions, not numbered upgrade levels.

### A foothold

One viable craft route operates inside a neglected estate.

### A functioning house

The complete production path works reliably enough to sustain releases.

### A house with direction

Investments begin expressing a deliberate craft and business identity.

### An established institution

Named people carry standards, important relationships depend on the finca, and the estate can withstand ordinary setbacks.

### A living legacy

The property contains years of crops, releases, people, experiments, improvements, and remembered events.

The player never needs to reach maximum physical size. A tiny, revered atelier can represent complete success.

## Vertical-slice scope

The vertical slice should not implement the entire facility vocabulary. It needs:

- One leaf-inspection and conditioning station
- One personal rolling workbench
- One small resting or aging space
- One office or notebook interface supporting intent, records, and release
- A compact, walkable graybox connecting those interactions
- Authored leaf whose provenance carries upstream growing, curing, and fermentation history

The slice proves intent, inspection, prototype construction, evaluation, revision, tiny-batch commitment, aging, and release. Farming, curing, and full fermentation can enter later milestones once the central craft-and-feedback loop works.

This implementation order is scope control, not a change to the final game's identity.

## Conceptual entities

Implementation should preserve distinct state for:

- Estate and authored expansion site
- Facility or zone
- Facility capability and operating state
- Workstation
- Environmental observation and trend
- Material load and comfortable capacity
- Operating policy
- Inspection, warning, and intervention
- Construction or reconfiguration project
- Maintenance plan and repair need
- Estate-wide infrastructure capability
- Staffing responsibility and scheduled attention
- Physical history or visible estate change

Exact data structures remain technical design work. These concepts must not be collapsed into one estate level or one universal facility-quality score.

## Design constraints

- Never turn the finca into an unrestricted factory-building grid.
- Never require a separate minigame for every represented production function.
- Never make raw acreage or building count the primary measure of success.
- Never require repetitive cleaning, repair, wiring, plumbing, or construction chores.
- Never hide catastrophic inventory loss behind an unreadable instant failure.
- Never let extra floor space create useful output without people, equipment, standards, and demand.
- Use soft capacity where risk and tradeoff are more interesting than hard blocking.
- Make upgrades solve identifiable limitations and produce visible change.
- Preserve the physical journey and provenance of important material without demanding logistics optimization.
- Allow relationships and outside services to substitute for owning every capability.
- Keep routine operation schedulable and delegable.
- Prove the central cigar-development loop before implementing the entire estate pipeline.

## Open calibration

Prototypes must determine:

- The exact authored finca layout and supported repurposing
- Environmental ranges, trend rates, and warning timing
- Comfortable capacity and overload effects
- Project costs, durations, and disruption
- Maintenance frequency and operating-cost balance
- How much workstation arrangement affects workflow
- The first implementation's visible estate states
- Which upstream spaces are explorable before their full simulations exist

Calibration may simplify presentation and combine functions. It must preserve meaningful facility decisions, a compact finca, and the game-first abstraction rule.
