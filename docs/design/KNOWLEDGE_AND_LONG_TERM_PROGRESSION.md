# Knowledge and long-term progression

**Status:** Canonical direction; exact confidence, skill, and pacing values remain open
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rules

> The player does not level up. Their understanding, personal technique, people, house, access, reputation, and history develop separately.

> Progression makes more of the world understandable and intentionally manageable; it does not make every number rise together.

Leaf & Ember replaces a universal experience level and linear technology tree with evidence, practice, institutional capability, relationships, and accumulated history.

## Progression dimensions

| Dimension | What develops it | What it provides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player knowledge | Observation, comparison, tasting, diagnosis, records, and teaching | Better vocabulary, predictions, interpretations, and causal confidence |
| Personal technique | Meaningful craft, correction, demonstration, and method practice | Greater control, consistency, and recovery during hands-on work |
| Artisan capability | Practice, mentorship, responsibility, and specialization | Independent work, distinct mastery, teaching, and leadership |
| House capability | Facilities, standards, records, equipment, inventory, and coordination | Work the organization can perform reliably |
| Access and relationships | Trust, fulfilled commitments, introductions, and shared history | Leaf, expertise, people, markets, commissions, and opportunities |
| Reputation and legacy | Released work, conduct, continuity, people, and time | A recognizable identity and persistent history others respond to |

Money, land, inventory, and production volume are resources or constraints. They are not character levels.

## Necessary separations

- Knowing that a wrapper is too dry does not grant the physical technique to apply it perfectly.
- A talented artisan does not improve the whole house unless their capability is taught, recorded, or supported by others.
- Purchasing an instrument provides observation or control; it does not grant understanding.
- High reputation creates opportunity and expectation; it does not improve the next cigar.
- A close relationship can provide honest advice or unusual access; it does not transfer mastery instantly.
- A written recipe preserves intent and procedure; it does not replace material, technique, facilities, or judgment.

These distinctions prevent one success track from becoming a universal solution.

## Player knowledge

### Core rule

> Knowledge changes what the player can observe, predict, and explain. It does not secretly improve the tobacco.

The physical material and process do not receive bonuses because the player has accumulated experience. Knowledge affects interpretation, available action, warning quality, and confidence.

### Knowledge cycle

```text
encounter
  -> observe
  -> form a hypothesis
  -> test or compare
  -> gather supporting or conflicting evidence
  -> apply the conclusion
  -> record or teach it
```

Failure can provide valuable evidence. A diagnosed flawed cigar may teach more than repeatedly producing safe work without comparison.

### Three knowledge layers

#### Craft literacy

General concepts, vocabulary, methods, instruments, and possible interventions.

The player begins with enough practical literacy to use the interface and understand basic goals. Essential information must not be hidden to manufacture progression. Mentors, demonstrations, reference material, equipment, and practice broaden the vocabulary and available methods.

#### Contextual familiarity

Knowledge of a specific subject, such as:

- A field plot across several seasons
- A grower's practices and related harvests
- A finite crop or leaf lot
- The curing barn under different weather
- A fermentation method or aging environment
- A recipe across several prototypes and batches
- An artisan's working tendencies
- A buyer's audience and expectations

Familiarity transfers only where the contexts are meaningfully related.

#### Evidence-based conclusions

Specific interpretations can include:

- A leaf lot adds sweetness at one proportion but becomes harsh when pushed further.
- A room tends to dry unevenly under particular conditions.
- A construction fault is more likely caused by bunching than leaf condition.
- A buyer's audience responds to a style but resists its current price.
- An artisan handles one vitola consistently but struggles with fragile wrapper.

These conclusions can be incomplete, contradicted, or refined.

### Knowledge record

A meaningful conclusion can retain:

- Subject and context
- Direct observations
- Supporting and conflicting evidence
- Information source
- Conditions under which it was observed
- Current qualitative confidence
- Related lots, recipes, people, facilities, and events
- The player's recorded interpretation

Confidence is local to a conclusion. A useful presentation may move through:

```text
unfamiliar -> noticed -> suspected -> supported -> dependable
```

Dependable means reliable within known conditions, not universal truth. Exact labels and thresholds require interface prototypes.

### Player-facing effects

Knowledge can provide:

- More precise qualitative descriptions
- Narrower prediction ranges
- Earlier recognition of trends
- Better-supported likely causes
- More useful comparisons
- Improved substitution forecasts
- Better warnings before time advancement
- Clearer production instructions and teaching standards

Knowledge never reveals an omniscient flavor report or the simulation's hidden values. Sensory observation remains shaped by the cigar, taster, and tasting context as defined in [`FLAVOR_AND_SENSORY.md`](FLAVOR_AND_SENSORY.md).

### The notebook

The notebook automatically records:

- Provenance and lot identity
- Player and artisan actions
- Process history and measurements
- Recipe and standard versions
- Construction and inspection results
- Tasting observations and diagnostic hypotheses
- Character feedback
- Release and market outcomes

The player contributes intent, relevant observations, and interpretations without performing clerical data entry after every action. Summaries expose useful patterns and retain links to the evidence behind them.

Personal work produces especially rich direct observations. Delegated work produces reports according to the responsible character's capability, instructions, instruments, and recordkeeping. This preserves the value of hands-on attention without making routine personal work mandatory forever.

Knowledge does not decay arbitrarily. A conclusion can become outdated, contradicted, or inappropriate for a new crop, but the game preserves what was previously believed and why.

## Personal technique and artisan capability

### Core rule

> Technique improves control over difficult work; it does not add an invisible quality bonus afterward.

A skilled craftsperson receives clearer feedback, makes more precise adjustments, works within demanding tolerances, and recovers from mistakes more effectively. Suitable material, sound judgment, and appropriate facilities remain necessary.

### Capability profile

The player and named artisans can develop specific capabilities across:

- Leaf inspection and grading
- Conditioning and preparation
- Bunching and draw control
- Wrapper application and finishing
- Particular vitolas and construction methods
- Quality control and fault diagnosis
- Sensory evaluation
- Fermentation and aging stewardship
- Teaching and workshop leadership

Related experience may transfer partially. Mastery of one method does not create a universal craft level.

### Learning

Technique develops through:

- Focused practice
- Meaningful feedback
- Correcting a diagnosed fault
- Demonstration and mentorship
- Comparing methods
- Handling unfamiliar or demanding material
- Increasing responsibility
- Teaching another person

Mindlessly repeating an easy action eventually provides little development. Routine work supports consistency; deliberate challenge, correction, and reflection create stronger learning.

### Player execution

The player's real input and the founder's developed technique work together. Improved capability can provide:

- Clearer tactile or visual cues
- More forgiving control without automatic success
- Better opportunities to correct a developing mistake
- Greater consistency across repeated work
- More efficient handling of familiar methods
- Access to fragile materials or demanding techniques

Accessibility assistance remains available independently of progression. It can simplify physical execution while preserving material, construction, and diagnostic decisions.

### Artisan execution

Artisan results emerge from:

```text
material condition
+ specification clarity
+ relevant capability and familiarity
+ facilities and tools
+ attention, workload, and confidence
= execution result and variation
```

Artisans use the same underlying craft model as the player. They do not roll against one employee-skill value or contribute arbitrary production bonuses.

### Training

Training uses time, material, and attention:

1. Demonstration introduces or reframes a method.
2. Supervised work reveals errors and habits.
3. Focused feedback addresses a specific cause.
4. Independent trials establish confidence.
5. Review determines readiness for responsibility.

Different characters can respond to different teaching methods. Mastery does not remove all variation. Fatigue, unfamiliar work, or poor conditions can temporarily reduce performance, while established permanent capability does not randomly decay.

## From personal method to house capability

### Core rule

> The house can reliably perform only what its people, records, facilities, and materials collectively support.

One exceptional result demonstrates possibility. It does not prove repeatable house production.

### Institutionalization path

A specific method can progress through:

```text
demonstrated
  -> personally repeatable
  -> documented
  -> validated under varied conditions
  -> taught to another person
  -> independently reproducible
  -> established as a house standard
```

This state belongs to a method, recipe, or capability rather than the whole house. Established portfolio work can coexist with personal experiments and uncertain prototypes.

### Capability supports

Reliable house work can require:

- A recorded recipe or process
- Clear tolerances and inspection standards
- Suitable facilities and instruments
- Available leaf or tested substitutions
- At least one trained person
- Reference samples and prior batch history
- Scheduled attention and quality control
- Known failure signs and recovery options

Missing support constrains the capability instead of switching it off without explanation. The player may wait, substitute, retrain, outsource, reduce scope, or return the work to development.

### Institutionalization loop

1. Record the method and intended result.
2. Repeat it enough to understand tolerances.
3. Identify common faults and diagnostic signs.
4. Teach it to a named artisan.
5. Supervise independent work.
6. Compare results and revise the specification.
7. Approve the method for house use.
8. Continue reviewing real batch evidence.

The time and tobacco spent here make delegation earned and legible.

### House archive

The archive preserves:

- Recipe and process versions
- Named contributors and teachers
- Reference cigars and leaf samples
- Specifications and tolerances
- Batch results and rejected approaches
- Tasting and market feedback
- Facility conditions and equipment
- Known substitutions and consequences
- Reasons a method changed or was retired

Standards retain authorship. An artisan's contribution remains visible rather than becoming an anonymous founder achievement.

### Knowledge has a location

Capability can reside in:

- The founder's personal experience
- An artisan's tacit technique
- Written records
- Physical reference material
- Shared workshop practice
- Facilities and instruments
- Relationships with outside experts

If an artisan leaves, documentation and trained colleagues remain. The house may still lose diagnostic depth, teaching ability, capacity, or exceptional execution. Nothing disappears arbitrarily, but dependence on one person is an understandable vulnerability.

### Standards remain revisable

A house standard is intentionally reproducible, not objectively final. The player can:

- Revise and version it
- Preserve approved variants
- Permit a temporary substitution
- Return it to development
- Retire it without deleting history
- Produce atelier work outside it

Dependability and experimentation remain compatible.

## Access and opportunities

### Core rule

> Opportunities open because the player created credible conditions, not because the house reached a required level.

An opportunity can depend on:

- Relevant knowledge or technique
- Suitable facilities
- Available material
- Money or capacity
- Trust with a person
- Reputation with a relevant audience
- A prior release, experiment, or event
- Calendar timing and finite availability

Meeting the conditions creates the opportunity; it does not guarantee success.

### How capabilities enter play

- **Learned:** A person demonstrates or explains a method that still requires practice.
- **Discovered:** The player observes, compares, experiments, or diagnoses.
- **Purchased:** Equipment, instruments, reference material, or facilities provide control or information.
- **Taught into the house:** Existing knowledge becomes shared practice.
- **Relationship-gated:** Trust provides introductions, candor, private information, collaboration, or unusual access.
- **Reputation-gated:** Demonstrated identity attracts relevant people, commissions, invitations, and markets.

### Multiple paths

Important capabilities should rarely have one exclusive source. A difficult fermentation method might be developed by learning from a specialist, hiring experience, experimenting with improved instruments, collaborating with another grower, or temporarily using an outside facility.

Routes differ in cost, time, risk, relationships, and how deeply knowledge becomes institutionalized. A damaged relationship can close that person's unique story or offer without permanently removing an essential game system.

### Opportunities appear through the world

- A grower mentions an unusual finite lot.
- An artisan proposes an experiment.
- A buyer describes an unmet need.
- A facility limitation makes better control relevant.
- A critic identifies a recurring weakness.
- A city craftsperson offers a new presentation option.
- A successful release produces an introduction.
- Old notes become newly useful when complementary leaf appears.

The journal can track known possibilities, preparation, and context. The game should not display a giant grid of locked nodes with house-level requirements.

### Access remains contextual

Rare leaf stays seasonal and finite. Buyers, artisans, invitations, and commissions have their own interests and timing. Trust does not guarantee supply, and reputation brings greater expectations alongside opportunity.

Progress expands a network of possibilities without turning it into a permanent universal catalog.

## Setbacks and continuity

Different progression dimensions respond differently to setbacks:

- Recorded knowledge and history remain unless the player deliberately loses material records through a clearly telegraphed event.
- A hypothesis can be contradicted without erasing the evidence that created it.
- Technique remains, while fatigue or unfamiliar work can affect current performance.
- House capability can become constrained when people, materials, facilities, or standards are unavailable.
- Access can change with supply, timing, conduct, and relationships.
- Reputation can strengthen, fragment, or recover across audiences.
- A failed crop or release becomes part of the archive rather than deleting long-term progress.

Setbacks create new decisions and history. They do not arbitrarily remove accumulated mastery.

## Founder and succession scope

The base game remains centered on one founder.

- There is no biological-family, heir, or generational succession system.
- Romance, dating, and marriage remain outside scope.
- The founder has no hard lifespan timer or forced retirement.
- The game can span many compressed years without simulating every aspect of aging and death.
- Other characters still experience career changes, mastery, departure, independence, and authored life events.

Legacy is carried by the house, work, people, and relationships rather than requiring a bloodline. Playable succession is reserved as a possible future expansion only if the completed game demonstrates a genuine need for it.

## The living legacy

The persistent historical record can include:

- Crops and exceptional harvests
- Purchased lots and grower relationships
- Recipe families and version history
- Inaugural, failed, defining, and retired releases
- Long-aged reserves
- Facility restoration and estate changes
- Artisans, teachers, apprentices, and collaborators
- Important contracts and commitments
- Critical and commercial responses
- Character memories and shared events

The finca physically expresses this history through boxes, samples, photographs, records, tools, releases, rooms, and people.

## Legacy paths

Legacy is not one score. The house can be remembered for:

- Defining estate tobacco
- A small body of exceptional atelier work
- A dependable and recognizable portfolio
- Experimental processing or blending
- Developing respected artisans
- Fair and enduring grower relationships
- Restoring a neglected finca
- Making excellent work accessible to a broader audience
- One legendary release that was never reproduced

These identities can overlap without requiring universal completion.

## Long-term milestones

Milestones support reflection, recognition, and authored events:

- The first house-branded release
- The first returning buyer
- The first independently reproduced recipe
- The first artisan becoming a master or mentor
- The first important aged reserve
- A house anniversary
- A former apprentice returning as a peer
- A release that helps define the house
- The finca becoming financially and operationally resilient

Milestones do not award universal victory points or end the save.

## Continuing play

After significant legacy milestones, the player can continue operating, pursue another specialty, mentor new artisans, revisit old recipes, open reserves, accept ambitious commissions, reduce production, or return to atelier work.

An optional reflective legacy sequence can summarize what the player built without deleting or permanently ending the save.

## Vertical-slice requirements

The progression portion of the vertical slice should prove that the player can:

1. Record intent and evidence for a prototype.
2. Compare at least two versions and refine a hypothesis.
3. Receive information whose precision reflects source and familiarity.
4. Improve or demonstrate one specific technique without gaining a global level.
5. Preserve a versioned recipe and its contributors.
6. Turn a personal result into the beginning of a teachable specification.
7. Gain one opportunity through credible preparation or relationship history.
8. Add the inaugural release and response to a persistent house record.

The slice does not need a mature skill economy, multi-year career, or complete legacy event library.

## Conceptual entities

Implementation should preserve distinct state for:

- Knowledge subject
- Observation and evidence record
- Hypothesis or conclusion
- Source, context, and qualitative confidence
- Prediction and uncertainty
- Individual capability profile
- Method familiarity and teaching history
- House capability and its current supports
- Versioned recipe, process, and standard
- Reference sample
- Archive entry and contributor credit
- Opportunity and contextual requirements
- Legacy event and historical relationship

Exact data structures and formulas remain technical design work. These concepts must not be collapsed into one experience, mastery, technology, house, or legacy level.

## Design constraints

- Never add a universal player, artisan, house, or legacy level.
- Never make repetition without feedback the optimal way to learn.
- Never let knowledge modify hidden material physics.
- Never reveal omniscient simulation truth as a progression reward.
- Never make purchased equipment equivalent to understanding.
- Never let one relationship permanently gate an essential system.
- Never erase knowledge or mastery through arbitrary decay.
- Never make a written standard sufficient without people, material, facilities, and validation.
- Preserve contributor credit and the location of important knowledge.
- Keep accessibility assistance independent from character progression.
- Let standards remain versioned, revisable, and compatible with atelier experimentation.
- Let failure, retirement of a product, and departure become history rather than deleted progress.
- Keep the founder playable without a lifespan deadline or required generational succession.

## Open calibration

Prototypes must determine:

- Exact confidence language and evidence weighting
- Number and granularity of capability domains
- How developed technique affects controls and feedback
- Learning pace and diminishing returns for familiar work
- Report detail from delegated tasks
- The interface connecting notebook, archive, standards, and opportunities
- The amount of validation required before reliable house production
- Long-term milestone cadence and calendar compression
- The first implementation's legacy presentation

Calibration may combine categories and simplify presentation. It must preserve evidence-based knowledge, specific capability, multiple opportunity paths, institutional resilience, and founder-centered legacy.
