# Leaf economy and terroir

**Status:** Canonical direction
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rule

> The farm gives the player a voice, not a complete vocabulary.

The player's land produces a characteristic range of tobacco. It does not produce one immutable flavor, and it cannot be manipulated into expressing every profile available from every growing region.

This limitation is productive. It makes the finca part of the house's identity while making other growers essential to blending.

## What shapes a leaf lot

A lot's eventual character and utility emerge from interacting influences:

- Region, field, soil, elevation, climate, and season
- Seed or cultivar
- Sun exposure or shade-grown method
- Plot preparation, nutrition, water, plant density, and crop health
- Position on the plant and harvest timing
- Curing conditions and handling
- Sorting and grading decisions
- Fermentation history
- Resting and aging history

Terroir creates tendencies rather than hard permissions. Cultivation and processing can express, moderate, damage, or redirect those tendencies.

## Finca profile

The player's plots should have legible agricultural tendencies, potentially including:

- Likely strength range
- Broad aroma and taste tendencies
- Leaf thickness and elasticity
- Combustion behavior
- Wrapper potential
- Yield range
- Weather and disease vulnerabilities

The detailed sensory vocabulary and the degree to which these traits are visible are deferred to the flavor-profile design.

## Crop disposition

Harvest does not convert directly into money or generic inventory. After curing and grading, each lot requires an allocation decision:

- **Reserve:** Keep it for future house blends.
- **Sell:** Sell cured and graded bales to a buyer, broker, or another house.
- **Process further:** Ferment or age it before sale, accepting time, space, and process risk for greater control and potential value.
- **Trade:** Exchange it directly for a complementary lot.
- **Commit:** Protect it for an established cigar's future production.
- **Select:** Reserve an exceptional portion as an estate or experimental selection.

The expected early sale point is after curing, sorting, and grading. More advanced sale and contract structures can become available as the player develops facilities and relationships.

## Lots, not commodities

Leaf is tracked as provenance-rich lots rather than interchangeable units. A lot should retain at least:

- Grower, farm, field, and region
- Seed or cultivar
- Crop and harvest dates
- Plant position or priming
- Cultivation method
- Curing, fermentation, and aging history
- Grade, quantity, condition, and observed defects
- Known sensory observations
- Purchase, contract, or relationship history

Availability is seasonal and finite. A valued lot cannot be repurchased forever from a universal catalog.

## Named growers and access

Purchased tobacco comes through people. Growers differ in land, practices, reliability, specialty, and willingness to collaborate. Access to their best lots may depend on:

- Trust and payment history
- Advance contracts
- Shared experiments
- Willingness to buy less glamorous portions of a crop
- Reputation for treating provenance honestly
- Personal or professional relationships
- Competition from other houses

The player can visit other farms to understand the tobacco and the people behind it. Sourcing should feel like building a network of expertise, not shopping from a stat list.

## Economic loop

Selling estate tobacco gives the player cash and relationships with which to acquire complementary leaves. The key cycle is:

```text
grow -> cure -> grade -> allocate -> sell or retain
     -> source complementary lots -> blend -> release
     -> earn money, knowledge, access, and reputation
```

This produces difficult choices:

- Sell wrapper-grade leaf now to fund a repair, or reserve it for a future premium release?
- Keep the entire crop for continuity, or sell while demand is unusually high?
- Buy a rare complementary lot, or redesign around something attainable?
- Sell early for cash flow, or occupy fermentation capacity to add value?
- Sell a celebrated lot to a competing house?
- Accept a stable contract that limits future allocation freedom?

## House identity

Estate leaf can become the recurring backbone or signature of the player's portfolio while sourced leaf changes around it. A house might become known for including some finca tobacco in every release without pretending the finca can provide every wrapper, binder, and filler the blender needs.

Producing entirely estate-grown or single-origin cigars is a meaningful creative constraint and possible prestige project, not the universal definition of quality.

## Balance constraints

- Purchased tobacco must not form a ladder of strictly superior ingredients.
- Growing everything personally must not be the optimal endpoint.
- Selling the entire crop and buying replacements must carry costs in margin, control, continuity, and identity.
- High yield must not automatically outrank exceptional small lots.
- Processing longer must not guarantee higher value; poor handling can damage leaf.
- Market demand, scarcity, relationships, and intended use all affect value.
- Improving the finca should deepen control and expression rather than primarily unlock enormous acreage.

## Research anchors

- [Zoning of tobacco leaf produced in northern Nicaragua](https://camjol.info/index.php/elhigo/article/download/8993/11847?inline=1) - regional differences in sensory attributes, texture, combustion, and the importance of fermentation
- [Habanos: shade-grown tobacco](https://www.habanos.com/en/tabaco-tapado-shade-grown/) - cultivation by intended function, curing, early processing, and transfer from grower to leaf-processing organization
- [University of Kentucky: Connecticut Broadleaf production](https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/agr-258) - an example of a known cigar cultivar grown in another suitable region, supporting a flexible rather than deterministic land model
