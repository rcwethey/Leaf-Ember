# Setting and narrative

**Status:** Canonical direction; final names, cast writing, and cultural review remain production work
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core rules

> Leaf & Ember takes place in a fictional valley and local town within real northern Nicaragua, connected to the real regional importance of Estelí.

> Fictional places provide creative freedom; they do not excuse generic or interchangeable cultural representation.

The setting should feel particular because of ordinary work, material history, language, geography, relationships, and community life. It must not use a generalized Central American aesthetic as a substitute for research.

## Geographic frame

### Real national context

Nicaragua is the real country surrounding the story. Its identity, language, geography, history, institutions, and contemporary tobacco economy provide the setting's frame.

The game does not invent a fictional country merely to avoid cultural responsibility. National context must be researched, and fictional content must remain compatible with it.

### Fictional primary locations

The following are fictional and authored for play:

- The highland valley
- The local town or municipality
- The finca and its history
- Neighboring farms
- Principal characters and families
- Cigar houses, growers, buyers, and most businesses
- Story-specific organizations and events

This gives the team freedom to create compact travel, land histories, property relationships, and dramatic events without claiming that they occurred to real people or businesses.

The game should plainly identify its central places and characters as fictional. It should not imply that a fictional valley is a hidden documentary representation of one real community.

### Estelí as regional anchor

Estelí exists as the real regional center connecting the valley to larger industry, commerce, services, exhibitions, finance, and export relationships.

The game represents Estelí selectively through:

- Correspondence and contacts
- Occasional authored visits or meeting locations
- Industry events
- Buyers, exporters, equipment, and services
- News and market connections

Leaf & Ember does not need to construct the whole city as an open world. Any directly represented district or interior must receive focused visual and cultural research.

### Place naming

Final valley, town, farm, business, and character names remain unset until a naming pass uses:

- Nicaraguan geographic and naming patterns
- Spanish-language review
- Regional history and terminology
- Checks against unintended real-world duplication
- Cultural consultation

Names should not be chosen merely because they sound generally Latin American. Placeholder names remain visibly provisional in production data.

## Contemporary period

The game uses a deliberately undated contemporary period. Its calendar displays Year 1, Year 2, and later years instead of tying the story to a real starting date.

The world supports:

- Mobile phones, messaging, and email
- Ordinary internet access and digital records
- Contemporary environmental instruments
- Modern banking, shipping, and export logistics
- Printed and online industry coverage
- Modern packaging and production equipment
- Traditional handcraft continuing alongside current tools

The game is not a historical reenactment. Modern technology supports communication, information, and coordination without replacing embodied craft.

### Stable modernity

An unnamed year allows a long-running save without requiring the game to simulate changing phone models, real elections, current exchange rates, pandemics, or live legal updates.

Weather, trade, and industry events can be fictional or systemic. They must be plausible within the setting without claiming to recreate current events.

### Communication scope

Contemporary communication allows the player to:

- Arrange appointments
- Receive photographs and reports
- Review offers and orders
- Hear from distant buyers
- Receive warnings from staff
- Maintain relationships without constant travel

There is no social-media-management minigame. Reputation develops through work, people, markets, reviews, and events rather than follower counts.

## The founder

The founder is customizable in:

- Name and pronouns
- Appearance
- House name and visual identity
- Prior work background
- Dialogue attitudes and repeated conduct

The founder is not a culturally blank tourist arriving to discover tobacco. Every supported background provides a credible connection to Nicaragua and prior experience within its agricultural, craft, quality, or commercial world. The founder begins capable of participating but is not an established master.

### Backgrounds

| Background | Initial familiarity | Example connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workshop hand | Preparation and basic construction | A former colleague or craftsperson |
| Leaf worker | Fields, curing, sorting, or processing | A grower or processing contact |
| Quality and records assistant | Tasting, documentation, and standards | An evaluator or workshop supervisor |
| Commercial assistant | Buyers, orders, packaging, or export work | A buyer, printer, or commercial contact |

A background can provide:

- Initial contextual knowledge
- One or two existing relationships
- Different opening dialogue
- Familiarity with certain actions
- A few notebook observations or methods

It does not create a permanent class, universal stat bonus, exclusive ending, or locked core system. Every founder can learn the game's core capabilities through credible routes.

Background writing must avoid positioning the player as an outsider who teaches local characters the value of their own knowledge. The founder depends on named people whose expertise, interests, and agency exist independently of the player.

## Acquisition of the finca

The finca was a modest working property, never a famous dynasty or major brand. Its aging proprietor gradually reduced operations after financial strain and changing personal circumstances. Several buildings have been lightly used or closed for years, and maintenance has been deferred.

The founder acquires the property through:

- Personal savings
- A manageable financed purchase agreement
- A small operating reserve
- Scheduled obligations visible from the beginning

This is a deliberate risk rather than a surprise inheritance. It establishes financial pressure without beginning in unavoidable collapse.

### The former proprietor

The former proprietor lives nearby and remains a named character. They retain history and emotional attachment but no continuing authority over the finca.

The relationship can develop into:

- Respectful mentorship
- Friendly but opinionated distance
- Regret over how the estate changes
- Renewed pride in its restoration
- Methodological conflict
- A close, family-like bond

The former proprietor is neither an infallible tutorial voice nor a concealed villain. Their methods, memories, mistakes, and boundaries should remain human and specific.

### A new house

The property contains incomplete records, old tools, physical traces of prior work, and a small amount of recoverable or purchased tobacco. These remnants create history and early questions without granting an established reputation.

The player chooses a new house name. The old property history remains visible, but the founder earns the new house's identity from the inaugural release onward.

The opening premise is:

> The founder chooses to risk their savings and reputation on a neglected finca they believe can become something meaningful.

## Narrative structure

Leaf & Ember combines a compact authored spine, state-driven character arcs, and emergent house history.

### Layer 1: the founding story

The founding story introduces the full game identity without becoming an endless main quest.

#### The commitment

- Acquire and inspect the finca.
- Meet the former proprietor and nearby community.
- Stabilize the minimum workshop.
- Choose the new house name.

#### The first honest box

- Acquire complementary leaf.
- Develop and revise a prototype.
- Produce a tiny inaugural batch.
- Convince someone to consider it.
- Receive the first genuine response.

#### A house taking shape

- Begin the first estate crop.
- Work with an apprentice or collaborator.
- Establish the first repeatable house standard.
- Face tension between personal work and responsibility.
- Make early investments that express the house's direction.

#### The test of identity

- Respond to larger demand, scarce material, or financial pressure.
- Resolve an important conflict without one universally correct answer.
- Decide what the house will compromise and protect.
- Release work that begins defining its reputation.

After this spine, play is fully open-ended. Later legacy milestones can trigger authored reflection without forcing the save to end.

### Layer 2: character arcs

Named characters have authored stories triggered by relevant systemic conditions. Examples include:

- An apprentice approaching independent mastery
- A grower facing a difficult crop decision
- A buyer asking the house to compromise its identity
- The former proprietor reacting to changes at the finca
- A competitor proposing collaboration during mutual difficulty
- An artisan seeking credit, leadership, or independence

These arcs respond to actual work, memories, promises, facilities, relationships, and calendar state. They do not advance only because the player selects a quest marker.

Important conversations can be scheduled or revisited where credible. Characters do not wait indefinitely in one pose, and players are not punished for missing hidden brief windows.

### Layer 3: emergent house history

The simulation produces stories that were not prescribed exactly:

- A substitute leaf unexpectedly saves a release.
- A flawed batch finds a useful but less prestigious purpose.
- An apprentice masters a cigar the founder struggled to teach.
- A long-held reserve matters during a poor crop year.
- A trusted buyer helps the finca survive financial pressure.
- A rival's work changes how the founder understands their own.

The archive records these outcomes. Later dialogue and authored scenes can acknowledge important systemic history.

## Story triggering and failure

Narrative triggers can consider:

- Calendar and season
- Completed work and active processes
- Memories and commitments
- Relationship qualities
- Character goals and employment state
- Facility restoration
- Money, inventory, and contracts
- Releases, feedback, and reputation
- Prior story choices

The system selects an appropriate authored scene or state change; it does not generate unconstrained narrative prose.

Failure usually changes a story rather than deleting it. A missed order, rejected cigar, damaged relationship, or lost crop can create recovery scenes, alternate opportunities, and persistent history. Essential game systems retain alternate entry paths.

## Narrative conflict and tone

There is no central supervillain or world-saving plot. Antagonism emerges from:

- Conflicting values
- Material limitations
- Weather and time
- Financial commitments
- Scarce leaf
- Pride and authorship
- Growth changing relationships
- The difference between excellence and reproducibility

Competitors can be difficult without being evil. The former proprietor does not conceal a melodramatic conspiracy. Old records can reveal abandoned experiments, human mistakes, and unresolved relationships without turning the game into a thriller.

The central dramatic question is:

> What does the founder owe to the craft, the people, the land, and the name they are creating?

Dialogue can express an answer. The player's repeated conduct provides the real one.

## Language and voice scope

### English-first release

English is the development language and the only promised initial release language.

Characters in the valley and local town are canonically speaking Spanish. English presentation functions as a translation convention rather than implying that everyone locally speaks English for the founder's benefit.

There is no language-learning mechanic or recurring translation challenge.

### English writing standard

English dialogue should:

- Read naturally rather than imitate an accent
- Avoid broken grammar as shorthand for nationality or education
- Avoid decorative or excessive untranslated phrases
- Preserve culturally meaningful terms where context teaches them
- Use consistent forms of address and naming

Terms such as *finca*, *pilón*, *casa de tabaco*, *vitola*, and *chaveta* can remain when they carry useful specific meaning. A contextual glossary can support understanding without interrupting conversation.

Spanish should not be scattered through English dialogue merely to make characters sound Latin American.

### No voice-over

The base game has no planned character voice-over.

Dialogue is text-based. The audio scope focuses on music, weather, tools, agricultural and workshop activity, city ambience, and the changing sound of the finca.

This decision allows substantially more responsive writing without the casting, recording, editing, localization, lip-sync, and revision costs of voiced dialogue.

### Localization readiness

Spanish and other localization remain possible future work, not launch promises.

Player-facing text must still be externalized from the beginning. The interface should support accents, punctuation, pluralization, variable text length, and translator context so future localization does not require redesigning the game.

## Cultural research standard

The project maintains a cited setting-and-culture guide covering:

- Northern geography, climate, agriculture, and seasonal rhythms
- Tobacco work and the surrounding economy
- Architecture, materials, interiors, and infrastructure
- Names, forms of address, vocabulary, and regional language
- Work roles, labor conditions, gender, age, and professional status
- Food, music, faith, celebrations, and social spaces where story-relevant
- Local and national history relevant to places and characters
- Contemporary institutions, communications, and commercial practices

Research should explain why a creative choice belongs. It should not become a collection of visual motifs detached from context.

### Source hierarchy

Prefer a combination of:

1. Nicaraguan primary and official sources
2. Nicaraguan scholarship, museums, archives, journalism, and literature
3. Spanish-language regional sources
4. Academic and industry research with clear provenance
5. Firsthand consultation and review

Tourism material can identify places and public traditions but must not become the only account of everyday life.

### Cultural consultation

When production permits, compensate at least one consultant familiar with northern Nicaragua. Consultation should occur at meaningful checkpoints:

- Geographic and naming direction
- Founder and major character outlines
- Environment, architecture, props, and signage briefs
- Dialogue samples and social dynamics
- Tobacco labor and community representation
- Final narrative and visual review

A consultant must be able to challenge or change work, not merely approve a nearly finished product.

### Indie scope

The game does not attempt to represent all of Nicaragua. It concentrates on one fictional valley, one local town, selected Estelí connections, and a small recurring cast.

Fewer places portrayed with specificity are preferable to a broad tourism checklist. Ordinary homes, work, meals, disagreements, humor, aspiration, and community obligations should carry more of the setting than exposition.

### Historical and political boundaries

The contemporary game does not simulate live elections, current political crises, or changing real-world law as content updates. It also does not claim that the fictional valley exists outside Nicaraguan history.

Any story that directly uses political history, conflict, religion, ethnicity, Indigenous heritage, or another sensitive cultural subject requires focused sources and consultation. These subjects must not be used as decorative tragedy or generic atmosphere.

## Research and review records

Production should track:

- Source and access date
- Subject and intended creative use
- Whether the source is primary, scholarly, industry, journalistic, or promotional
- Confidence and unresolved contradictions
- Fictionalization decisions
- Consultant feedback and resulting changes
- Assets, characters, dialogue, or systems affected

Research notes inform creative work but do not become player-facing encyclopedia entries by default.

## Vertical-slice requirements

The setting and narrative portion of the vertical slice should prove that the player can:

1. Understand that the finca is in a fictional northern Nicaraguan valley.
2. Select or receive a credible founder background.
3. Inspect the neglected property and understand the financed acquisition.
4. Choose the new house name.
5. Meet at least three locally grounded named characters.
6. Complete the first-box story through work rather than quest-only dialogue.
7. Trigger one character response from systemic history.
8. Read natural English text without voice-over or decorative accent writing.
9. Experience a compact environment supported by cited visual research.

The slice does not need the final town, a full representation of Estelí, every background, Spanish localization, voice assets, or the entire founding narrative.

## Conceptual entities

Implementation should preserve distinct state for:

- Setting definition and fiction boundary
- Place and travel connection
- Founder background
- Acquisition agreement and opening obligations
- Narrative thread and stage
- Authored scene and systemic trigger
- Story choice, consequence, and recovery state
- Character arc and scheduling state
- House-history reference used by dialogue
- Localized text entry and terminology context
- Research source and cultural review record

Exact data structures remain technical design work. Story content should use explicit state and authored responses rather than a hidden universal narrative score.

## Design constraints

- Never present the valley as a generic stand-in for all of Central America.
- Never use a fictional country to avoid researching Nicaragua.
- Never imply that fictional characters, companies, or events represent real people without authorization.
- Never select final Spanish names or dialogue solely by intuition or machine translation.
- Never make the founder an outsider who supplies all valuable knowledge to local people.
- Never make cultural identity a collection of props, food, accents, and festivals detached from ordinary life.
- Never imitate accents through broken English.
- Never require Spanish fluency from the player.
- Never promise character voice-over for the base game.
- Never make live politics, social-media metrics, or current legal paperwork the core simulation.
- Keep the authored story grounded in work, relationships, and material consequences.
- Let failure branch, complicate, and enter history rather than simply ending a quest.
- Preserve open-ended play after the founding spine and legacy reflection.
- Require cultural review before finalizing high-cost narrative and environment content.

## Research anchors

- [Visit Nicaragua: Estelí Department](https://www.visitanicaragua.com/esteli/) - official regional overview and Estelí's connection to surrounding valleys and the tobacco industry
- [INETER Climate Atlas of Nicaragua](https://webserver2.ineter.gob.ni/mapas/Nicaragua/clima/atlas/index.html) - official temperature, precipitation, humidity, and climate maps for environmental reference
- [Nicaraguan Institute of Culture: Estelí cultural map](https://www.inc.gob.ni/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ESTEL%C3%8D-31-03-23.pdf) - official inventory of selected cultural, architectural, artistic, and landscape references
- [ASALE presentation of the Dictionary of Nicaraguan Spanish](https://www.asale.org/sites/default/files/Presentacion_diccionario_espanol_nicaragua_Julian_Corrales_Munguia_2007.pdf) - Nicaraguan vocabulary, forms, demonyms, and regional-language reference path
- [Central Bank of Nicaragua annual report 2025](https://www.bcn.gob.ni/publicaciones/informe-anual-2025-0) - contemporary national manufacturing and export context without hard-coding current figures
- [Nicaraguan Chamber of Tobacco overview](https://www.purosaborcigarfest.com/know-more/) - current industry organization and public-facing tobacco context

These sources establish research directions, not a complete cultural authority. Consultant review and additional Nicaraguan scholarship, literature, archives, and firsthand perspectives remain necessary.

## Open production work

- Final valley, town, finca, farm, business, and character names
- The compact geographic map and selective Estelí representation
- The former proprietor's identity and detailed history
- Final founder-background scenes and starting relationships
- The initial cast's biographies, speech, and social network
- Exact founding-story scenes, triggers, and alternate outcomes
- A maintained terminology and naming guide
- Consultant selection, review schedule, and compensation
- Visual research briefs for architecture, clothing, tools, signage, interiors, and landscape
- Future localization decisions after base-game scope and budget are proven

Production work can refine fictional specifics. It must preserve the real-Nicaragua frame, English-first text-only scope, cultural review standard, and three-layer narrative structure.
