# Tasting and diagnosis

**Status:** Canonical direction; exact interface remains open
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Core loop

```text
intent -> prototype -> observe -> compare feedback
       -> diagnose -> revise -> test again
```

Tasting is an act of inquiry. It is not a quiz in which the game eventually reveals the objectively correct list of flavors.

## Four records

### Design intent

Before construction, the player records the desired strength, body, dominant and supporting character, progression, finish, audience, occasion, and production constraints.

### Tasting journal

The journal records what the player perceived, where it occurred, how prominent it seemed, and how confident the observation was.

### Independent feedback

Named artisans, mentors, buyers, critics, or customers contribute their own observations. Agreement increases confidence; disagreement is information about sensitivity, preference, context, or inconsistency.

### Diagnosis

The player records a hypothesis about why the result diverged from the intent or approved specification and what should change in the next version.

## Focused tasting session

A prototype tasting takes place physically at a table on the finca, with a focused interface layered over the embodied scene.

1. Select a prototype and optional comparison cigar.
2. Review the design intent without exposing hidden results.
3. Inspect appearance, condition, aroma, and pre-light draw.
4. Move deliberately through the opening, middle, and final portions.
5. Observe draw, burn, smoke volume, heat, wrapper behavior, and sensory impressions.
6. Pin significant impressions to the tasting timeline.
7. Record strength, body, intensity, transitions, finish, and confidence.
8. Compare the journal with the intent and available external feedback.
9. Form a diagnosis and create or amend a prototype revision.

The interaction is calm and analytical. It must not require repeatedly pressing a puff button or waiting in real time for a full-length cigar.

## Communicating senses

Because the player cannot literally smell or taste the virtual cigar, the game supplies concise sensory impressions appropriate to the character's current knowledge. The player's agency lies in deciding which impressions are important, how they relate across the cigar, and what caused them.

The interface should not ask the player to type arbitrary prose or guess from no evidence. A controlled vocabulary supports comparison, localization, accessibility, and simulation feedback. Optional personal notes may exist outside the mechanical model.

## Hard data and subjective reports

Quality control can report measurable or directly observable evidence:

- Dimensions and weight
- Draw behavior
- Burn rate and evenness
- Smoke temperature trend
- Structural defects
- Variation within a batch

Sensory reports remain perspectives. The system may maintain a hidden expression for simulation consistency, but it does not display an omniscient actual-flavor report.

## Diagnosis

The player can connect evidence to one or more hypotheses, such as:

- Component ratio or dominance
- Insufficient or excessive fermentation
- Leaf or cigar aging state
- Tobacco condition at rolling
- Bunch density or airflow
- Vitola and combustion behavior
- Storage conditions
- Normal lot or batch variation

A diagnosis is tested through revision. An incorrect hypothesis costs time and material but produces new evidence; it is not an irreversible dialogue failure.

## Two kinds of variation

- **Design variation:** A prototype differs from its intended experience. Likely causes include leaf selection, processing, ratios, vitola, or aging.
- **Production variation:** Staff-made cigars differ from the approved prototype or one another. Likely causes include material condition, construction, tolerances, storage, or artisan consistency.

The game must label these problems differently so the player does not redesign a good blend to compensate for poor execution.

## Comparative and panel tasting

The player can later:

- Compare two prototype revisions side by side
- Taste a component or known reference alongside a blend
- Conduct blind comparisons to reduce expectation bias
- Invite an artisan panel
- Send samples to an outside evaluator
- Compare an aged batch with its younger record

Comparative tasting highlights tradeoffs rather than declaring one version the winner.

## Cost and pacing

Tasting consumes a cigar, advances part of the workday, and may require waiting for appropriate rest. These costs give experimentation weight without making it prohibitively expensive.

## Interface constraints

- Preserve the embodied finca setting.
- Keep the full session compact enough to repeat during development.
- Support mouse, controller, keyboard, readable text, and non-color-only cues.
- Separate observation from diagnosis in the layout.
- Keep intent, journal, external feedback, and revision history available for comparison.
- Never require precise real-world tobacco knowledge from the player before the game has taught it.
