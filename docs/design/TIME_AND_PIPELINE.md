# Time and production pipeline

**Status:** Canonical direction; numerical timing remains prototype calibration
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-02

## Purpose

Tobacco agriculture, curing, fermentation, aging, production, and reputation operate across very different time scales. The calendar must make patience meaningful without turning the game into waiting or a frantic race against midnight.

## Four scales of time

- **Minutes:** Inspecting leaf, rolling a prototype, tasting, diagnosing a problem, or speaking with a character
- **Days:** Training, maintaining facilities, selecting lots, planning production, hosting visitors, and fulfilling immediate work
- **Weeks:** Curing, fermentation cycles, established production, contracts, and shorter resting periods
- **Months and seasons:** Crops, aging programs, releases, market response, and the development of reputation

The exact ratios are a balancing question. Their relationship is canonical.

## Calendar model

The preferred direction is a deliberate, action-based workday:

- Important activities consume understandable portions of the day.
- Ordinary walking and looking around do not create constant pressure.
- The player deliberately ends the workday from their living quarters or office.
- Stable periods can be advanced by multiple days with a clear forecast and summary.
- Scheduled events, process checkpoints, and emerging risks interrupt long advances before preventable damage occurs.

The game should produce the feeling of using time wisely, not moving as quickly as possible.

## Daily blocks

> Time advances when the player commits to meaningful work, travel, or a scheduled event, not while walking, inspecting, reading, or navigating menus.

Each day contains three blocks:

1. Morning
2. Afternoon
3. Evening

A substantial activity normally consumes one block. Examples include rolling and evaluating prototypes, teaching, intervening in a sensitive process, fieldwork, visiting a grower, meeting a buyer, focused tasting, release planning, or supervising important construction.

Demanding work can consume two blocks or continue across several days. The game shows the cost and expected completion before commitment.

### Free observation and planning

These actions do not independently consume a block:

- Walking around the finca
- Inspecting visible objects and material
- Reading records and the journal
- Reviewing inventory and schedules
- Routine greetings
- Comparing recorded information
- Arranging appointments
- Changing plans before work begins

Curiosity and preparation should not create time pressure.

### Routine and passive work

Established processes, production, maintenance, and delegated work advance according to their schedule, staffing, standards, and facilities. The player spends personal time when judgment, experimentation, difficult material, teaching, risk, or chosen hands-on involvement makes attention meaningful.

### Ending a block

The player deliberately commits to and ends each block. Before evening ends, the game previews upcoming appointments, warnings, and commitments. Ending evening advances to the next day.

Evening supports tasting, records, social scenes, or hands-on work. Staff overtime is a separate employment decision with workload consequences. The founder does not use a conventional stamina bar.

## Year and climate

The prototype begins with twelve familiar months containing eight playable days each:

```text
12 months x 8 days = 96 days per year
```

This creates 288 possible attention blocks before time skips. The 96-day year is the initial implementation target and may be tuned after one complete crop and release cycle is playable.

### Climate periods

The calendar does not impose four temperate farming-game seasons. The fictional valley follows researched northern Nicaraguan climate tendencies through:

- A dry period
- Transition into rains
- A rainy period
- A possible drier interval within the rains
- Heavier or sustained rains
- Transition back toward drier conditions

Climate periods affect fields, curing, facility stability, roads, travel, construction, and crop risk. Month names remain useful for appointments, contracts, anniversaries, and history.

[`SETTING_AND_NARRATIVE.md`](SETTING_AND_NARRATIVE.md) governs the real geographic frame and climate-research standard.

### Annual variation

Years share climate tendencies without repeating identical weather. Transitions can be earlier, later, drier, wetter, steadier, or more erratic. Forecasts communicate tendencies and uncertainty. Variation creates adaptation without relying on unavoidable random destruction.

Early years contain more individually played days. Established houses increasingly advance through stable periods toward meaningful checkpoints and events.

## Travel and scheduling

> Travel consumes time as part of a meaningful visit; the player does not spend separate blocks watching a journey.

### Travel scale

- **Within the finca:** Movement between fields, barns, workshops, and living spaces is free.
- **Local town or neighboring farm:** A planned visit normally consumes one block, including travel, the primary activity, related conversation, and return.
- **Estelí:** A substantial trip normally consumes two blocks or a full working day. Several appointments can be bundled deliberately.
- **Distant markets:** Correspondence, visiting characters, reports, and shipments handle ordinary contact. Rare long-distance travel is an authored event.

A compact regional map or scheduled appointment shows time cost, financial cost, expected return, participants, weather or road concerns, and tasks that can be bundled. There is no driving or vehicle-maintenance minigame.

### Appointments

Appointments use morning, afternoon, evening, or a flexible day window rather than exact clock times. The player can arrange, confirm, renegotiate, or cancel them.

Renegotiating early differs from failing to appear. Consequences depend on the person, stakes, and relationship history rather than one universal missed-appointment penalty.

Characters can visit the finca, send information, request later meetings, attend shared events, or arrange for others to carry material. Relationships should not become a constant travel tax.

## Safe multi-day advancement

The player can advance:

- One day
- Several selected days
- To the next event
- To the next process checkpoint
- To a chosen calendar date

Before confirmation, the game previews known appointments, commitments, process checkpoints, weather concerns, construction, production, payments, financial obligations, character events, and work lacking staff or instructions.

### Automatic interruption

Advancement stops at the beginning of a block when:

- A meaningful decision is required.
- A process enters a warning state.
- An appointment or visitor arrives.
- A contract, payment, or serious forecasted shortfall needs attention.
- Weather invalidates an important plan.
- A character or narrative event becomes available.
- Assigned work can no longer continue safely.

The player receives the decision before preventable damage occurs. Advancing again after a warning deliberately accepts the stated risk.

### Simulation during advancement

- Assigned characters perform established work.
- Facilities follow recorded operating policies.
- Stable processes continue.
- Wages, costs, payments, and contracts advance.
- Crops and environmental conditions update.
- Relationships acknowledge fulfilled or missed commitments.
- Reports accumulate according to capability, instruments, and recordkeeping.

Unassigned work does not happen magically.

## The living pipeline

The antidote to empty waiting is overlapping work:

```text
current crop -> curing leaf -> fermenting lots -> resting tobacco
             -> developmental blends -> production batches
             -> aging cigars -> current releases -> market response
```

At maturity, the player is deciding what deserves attention across several years of accumulated work. A new crop can begin while an older harvest is being blended and an even older release is reaching customers.

## Process compression

Exact durations remain prototype data. Their relative scale is canonical:

| Scale | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Same block | Inspection, sample sorting, prototype rolling, immediate diagnosis |
| One or several days | Conditioning, prototype resting, small production, routine repair |
| Several days or parts of a month | Curing checkpoints, fermentation cycles, construction projects |
| Several months | Crop development, leaf resting, cigar aging, market response |
| Multiple years | Deep reserves, mature house history, artisan careers, legacy |

Processes move through meaningful states rather than only filling a timer. More time is never automatically better.

### Prototype starting ranges

Use these values to begin implementation, then tune them through play:

- Prototype resting: 1-3 days
- Tiny-batch production: 1-4 days
- Curing: approximately 1-2 compressed months
- Fermentation: several cycles across days or months
- Estate crop: approximately 4-6 compressed months
- Initial cigar aging: several weeks or months
- Long-aged releases: one or more in-game years
- Buyer feedback and payment: days to several months

The correct duration is the one that preserves patience while leaving the player another meaningful activity. Exact real-world duration is a research input, not an automatic game value.

## Attention and delegation

Routine work becomes delegable as the house grows. The player remains personally useful when:

- A field or batch behaves unexpectedly
- A critical process approaches a decision point
- Exceptional material deserves closer handling
- An artisan needs instruction
- The player is experimenting
- A high-risk or high-prestige commitment is underway

Delegation requires adequate staff, standards, facilities, and trust. It reduces repetitive actions; it does not eliminate consequences or information.

## Warnings, failures, and recovery

Tobacco should not become worthless because the player missed an invisible instant. Developing problems move through readable states, such as:

- A fermentation pile trending too hot
- Uneven humidity in a curing barn
- Rising mold risk
- Leaf losing workable elasticity
- An aging room drifting outside its intended range
- A production batch showing inconsistent draw

Warnings should provide enough information for diagnosis. Responses may preserve the batch, change its grade, redirect its use, or accept a tradeoff. Catastrophic loss is possible when risks are knowingly neglected, but it is not the default punishment for ordinary play.

## Time and knowledge

Experience improves foresight. Early players react to visible problems. Skilled players learn seasonal patterns, recognize early indicators, schedule inspections, and design facilities and teams that prevent recurring failures.

The calendar therefore becomes part of mastery rather than merely a countdown.

## Summaries and historical record

### Advancement summary

After a time skip, the game emphasizes:

- Work completed
- Material or process changes
- Warnings and interventions
- Money received or spent
- Important weather
- New messages and opportunities
- Relationship or character developments
- Knowledge added to the archive

Routine unchanged entries remain collapsed but available.

### Monthly review

Each compressed month creates an automatic operational snapshot covering:

- Cash and upcoming obligations
- Crops and active processes
- Inventory movement
- Production and releases
- Construction and maintenance
- Staff workload and development
- Appointments, commitments, warnings, and opportunities

The review is concise and optional rather than a mandatory score screen.

### Annual record

Each year becomes a chapter in the house archive. It can preserve:

- Climate and crop character
- Estate and purchased leaf
- Tobacco sold, retained, redirected, or lost
- Recipes developed, approved, revised, and retired
- Releases and important responses
- Financial changes
- Artisans joining, developing, teaching, or departing
- Facility changes
- Significant relationships and commitments
- Defining successes, failures, and unexpected events

The player can select mementos to display, but historical recording is automatic.

There is no universal annual grade. A financially difficult year can be creatively defining, while a stable profitable year may add little new craft knowledge.

### Historical timeline

The archive can filter by year, month, lot, recipe, release, character, facility, account, grower, commitment, or contract.

Important provenance and recipe evidence remains detailed. Routine daily logs can compact into monthly records as years accumulate, keeping saves and interfaces manageable.

Year-end review never forces retirement or pauses the house. Existing inventory, relationships, staff, facilities, contracts, and consequences continue into the next year.

## Design constraints

- Do not build the main loop around an always-running short day.
- Do not charge time for ordinary movement, inspection, reading, or menu navigation.
- Do not require repetitive manual care for hundreds of plants.
- Do not make time-skipping a blind gamble.
- Do not let every process complete simultaneously on a simple timer.
- Do not impose four temperate seasons on the Nicaraguan setting.
- Do not create a driving or vehicle-maintenance game around travel.
- Do not grade every month or year with one score.
- Preserve meaningful seasonal and long-aging decisions.
- Always show what will need attention before a deliberate time advance.

## Open calibration

- Which activities consume one or two blocks
- Whether 96 days produces the intended annual pace
- Exact process durations within the approved relative scales
- Warning thresholds and interruption timing
- Forecast accuracy at different knowledge levels
- Summary density and archive navigation
- Performance and save-data compaction for long-running houses

These values should be resolved through the Milestone 1 calendar prototype and later full-pipeline play rather than additional pre-implementation design gates.
